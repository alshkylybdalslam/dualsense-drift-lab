5 · Recalibrate (writes to controller)

What it does: asks the controller to re-measure where "resting" is, then stores that as the new centre. It does not repair the worn potentiometer — it recentres around the wear.

It is reversible. The default write is volatile: it lives in RAM and unplugging the controller restores the original calibration. So you can try it with no long-term risk. Tick "make permanent" only after it feels right.

Honest caveat: recentring steals throw from the drifting direction. With heavy drift that can cost accuracy on the healthy side. That's exactly why unplug-to-undo is the default.

Keep the stick untouched during Step 1.

Safety notice. Recalibration recentres your sticks around worn hardware; it does not repair the potentiometer. Writes are volatile by default — unplugging the controller restores the original calibration. The permanent write (report 0x80) unlocks controller flash and is not undone by unplugging. Everything you do here is logged on screen. Use at your own risk, and back up your calibration before writing.
